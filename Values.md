# Values

The human brain, like a government, has a system of laws that rule its conscious mind;
if these rules are broken, the unconscious makes the consciousness suffer,
and if these rules are met, the unconscious rewards the conscious mind.
This government has been called "**Superego**" by *Sigmund Freud*.

The term "**value**" suits these "rules" because they are primarily initiated from
**external rewards and punishments**; it's simple: external rewards and punishments
lead to internalised rewards and punishments.

Human children learn values primarily from their caregivers;
when a child is rewarded by kindness or delicious foods or
perceives aggression because of something bad it has done,
it internalises that as a value; a system of rights and wrongs.

As children grow, they can develop maturer kinds of rewards and punishments.
A profession, in its core, is a system of values.
Engineering, labour work, business, as simple as driving cars and as complicated as
conducting scientific experiments, they are all systems of rewards and punishments,
namely values. Even art is a system of values.

And the important thing is that these all can, in certain contexts,
lead one to psychological suffering...

### Psychological Pleasure & Pain

Psychological pleasure and pain are exclusively tied to:

**Values matching the external world**

The brain says my rules must match what I sense out there.
If they don't, I shall suffer!
Then the brain tries to change the world, tries to control the world around itself.
The more frequently and intensely the brain experiences "things that need change" and deals with them,
the more neurotransmitters and hormones of negative affect it induces.

These neurotransmitters, such as noradrenaline, if induces frequently, eventually lead one to suffer.
Suffering can come in various forms such as anxiety, anger, depression.
These are natural responses to the external world, and definitely not "disorders".

Human beings are the one species who changed the world!
And they couldn't do it without their anxieties, depression, sorrow, anger and hatred.
Humans are world-altering machines! It's their nature, not their "disorders".

## Psychological Relief

> Humans suffer their unrealistic beliefs and unmet expectations.

Happiness comes from **values matching one's environment**.
Therefore psychological relief, or as psychoanalysts call it "healing", should come from these ways:

1. **Changing one's environment**:
   This is the most effective way of achieving happiness.
   Living in an environment that matches your standards or at least doesn't torture you psychologically.
   But there are often one of these two problems:
     1. Changing one's environment might not be possible physically or economically.
     2. Some humans deliberately put themselves in environments to suffer because of their beliefs!
   But it doesn't change the fact that this is the most straight-forward way of relief from pain.
   People's lifestyles determine their level of happiness.
   This method is most effective on anxiety and anger.
   And you won't need to pay a penny to any psychologist!

2. **Changing one's values**:
   This is critically hard but is effective on depression, but not on anger or anxiety.
   Psychoanalysists and logotherapists utilise it.
   EMDR and other exposure methods try to change unconscious values and are effective on phobias.

3. **Changing how you perceive your environment** (cognitive restructuring):
   This is primarily utilised by cognitive-behavioural therapists and by logotherapists to some extent.
   It sometimes works and sometimes doesn't.
   It's the least effective one of them.

4. **Imitating happiness**:
   Not this is the one method that is almost "ineffective".
   Yes, it can release positive hormone temporarily, but it doesn't fix anything!
   It includes:
     - Deliberate laughter
     - Meditation
     - Mindfulness
     - Deep breathing
     - Muscle relaxation
   These are some stupid ways to calm your nerves if you can afford the above methods.

> In 99% of times, human beings ignorantly, persistently and unnecessarily,
> create and live their own suffering, all because of their instincts!
